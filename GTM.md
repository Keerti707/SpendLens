# Go-To-Market Plan

## Target User

The exact target user for SpendLens is a technical founder, engineering manager, or early finance/operator at a seed to Series A startup with 5–50 employees.

This user is not casually experimenting with AI tools. They already pay for multiple subscriptions such as ChatGPT Team, Claude, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Gemini, and API usage. Their team has adopted AI quickly, but nobody has clearly owned the cost structure yet.

The strongest initial segment is:

> Engineering-heavy startups with small teams, multiple AI subscriptions, and growing API usage.

This user cares about speed, but they also care about burn rate. They may not want a long procurement process, but they do want a fast second opinion on whether their AI spend is reasonable.

## What They Are Doing Before They Need SpendLens

Right before wanting this tool, they are likely:

- reviewing monthly SaaS expenses
- seeing a surprisingly high OpenAI or Anthropic API bill
- adding new team members to Cursor, Copilot, Claude, or ChatGPT
- preparing investor updates and burn-rate numbers
- trying to reduce runway pressure
- comparing AI tools after noticing overlap between subscriptions
- reading online discussions about AI tooling costs

Searches or thoughts they may have:

- “Why is our OpenAI bill so high?”
- “Cursor vs Copilot for teams”
- “ChatGPT Team worth it for startups”
- “How to reduce AI API costs”
- “AI tool stack for startup engineering teams”

## First 100 Users in 30 Days With $0 Budget

### Week 1: Founder-led outreach

I would personally DM 50–70 early-stage founders, indie hackers, and student founders with a very specific message:

> “I built a free AI spend audit tool. If you use ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Copilot, or API credits, it shows whether your stack has avoidable spend in under 2 minutes. No login needed.”

The goal is not mass promotion. The goal is to get 20 real audits and observe what inputs users enter.

### Week 2: Community launch

I would post a transparent build-in-public thread on X and LinkedIn:

- problem: startups do not know they are overspending on AI tools
- demo GIF
- example audit
- link to run a free audit
- ask founders to reply with their AI stack

I would also post in r/SaaS and Indie Hackers with a useful angle:

> “I analyzed common AI tool overspend patterns in small teams — here is the free calculator I built.”

### Week 3: Comparison content

I would publish small posts around practical search-intent topics:

- Cursor + Copilot overlap
- ChatGPT Team vs Plus for small teams
- Claude Pro vs Team
- When API credits matter
- How to audit AI spend before cutting engineering tools

Each post would link to SpendLens as the free audit.

### Week 4: Credex-led distribution

Credex’s unfair advantage is access to real discounted AI credit supply and knowledge of what teams are paying retail. Credex can use anonymized savings examples to create highly specific content:

> “A 7-person AI startup was paying retail for overlapping tools. Here is how much they could save.”

This is stronger than generic SEO because it is based on real market pain.

## Unfair Distribution Channel

The unfair channel is Credex’s existing positioning around discounted AI infrastructure credits.

Most generic SaaS spend tools cannot say:

> “We found overspend and can also help you source discounted credits.”

Credex can. That makes SpendLens both a diagnostic tool and a natural entry point into the Credex marketplace.

## Week-1 Traction Goal

If the launch works, week-1 traction should look like:

- 100 unique visitors
- 40 completed audits
- 15 captured leads
- 5 high-savings leads above $500/month
- 3 booked consultation conversations
- 5–10 users sharing report screenshots or links

The most important week-1 signal is not total traffic. It is whether users with meaningful AI spend complete the audit and willingly submit their email after seeing savings.
