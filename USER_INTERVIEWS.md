# User Interviews

These were some of the questions that were asked in the interview!

What AI tools do you use?
Do you pay for any of them? If yes, roughly how much?
Have you ever felt two AI tools you use overlap?
Would you use a free tool that tells you if you’re overspending?
Would you give your email after seeing the result to get a full report?
What would make you trust or not trust the audit?
What is one feature you’d want in this tool?

## Interview 1

**Name / initials:** Siya
**Role:** Student AI tool user  
**Company / project stage:** Personal and academic usage  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Duration:** ~10 minutes  

### Context

Siya uses multiple AI tools for personal and academic work, including ChatGPT, Gemini, and sometimes Claude. She has paid for ChatGPT once and had some awareness of tool overlap between different AI assistants.

### Direct Quotes from the interviewee

1. “I use ChatGPT, Gemini, and sometimes Claude.”
2. “I have actually felt two AI tools overlapping.”
3. “I would see if many users use it or if it’s legit. I will do the math myself too.”
4. “If it’s accurate then I will believe it.”
5. “I would like a feature which notifies me that you are overspending on these AI tools at every month end.”

### Most Surprising Thing They Said

The most surprising thing was that she would not blindly trust the audit result. She said she would manually verify the math for atleast one tool first and foremost and only trust the product if that calculation matched.

### What It Changed About My Design

This validated the decision to keep audit math rule-based and explainable instead of fully AI-generated. It also reinforced the need to show per-tool breakdowns, not just one final savings number. A future version should include monthly reminders or recurring audit notifications.

## Interview 2

**Name / initials:** Dhruv  
**Role:** Web Developer and Game Developer
**Company / project stage:** Personal learning and project usage  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Duration:** ~10 minutes  

### Context

Dhruv uses Claude and ChatGPT but does not currently pay for AI tools. His feedback was useful because he represents users who may not be paying yet but could become paid users later when their needs grow.

### Direct Quotes from the interviewee

1. “Claude and ChatGPT.”
2. “If I ever decide to spend then for sure.”
3. “If the project team is not at all transparent or the project is not open source, I would not trust it.”
4. “It can have a recommendation system where I give it my needs and my budget and it gives me advice on how to spend it on the right AI.”

### Most Surprising Thing They Said

The biggest trust factor for him was transparency. He specifically mentioned open source and project transparency, not just UI polish or number accuracy.

### What It Changed About My Design

This confirmed that the app should clearly explain how it calculates savings and should not behave like a black box. It also gave a good week-2 feature idea: a budget-based recommendation mode where the user enters their needs and budget, and SpendLens recommends the best AI stack.

## Interview 3

**Name / initials:** Astha
**Role:** Student / casual AI tool user  
**Company / project stage:** Personal productivity usage  
**Date:** 2026-05-12  
**Duration:** ~10 minutes  

### Context

Astha uses ChatGPT and Gemini but does not currently pay for them. She was more skeptical about the product because she does not think about AI spend only in money terms. Her feedback introduced a different angle: time and productivity cost.

### Direct Quotes from the interviewee

1. “ChatGPT and Gemini.”
2. “Sometimes otherwise no.”
3. “No.”
4. “If I get detailed honest statistics of the time I am spending on it.”
5. “It should include whether the time I am spending on the searches is productive or not.”

### Most Surprising Thing They Said

She did not find a money-only audit compelling because she is not currently paying for AI tools. Instead, she cared more about whether the time spent using AI tools was productive.

### What It Changed About My Design

This helped clarify the target audience. SpendLens is more valuable for teams already spending money on AI tools, not casual free-tier users. However, their feedback suggests a future feature: productivity-aware audits that combine spend data with usage quality or time saved.
